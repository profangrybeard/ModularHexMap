# ModularHexMap — Full Audit (2026-06-10)

> An outside review of the design, pipeline, tooling, and codebase, written after reading
> every doc, every C++ file in the plugin, all live Python tooling, the design records
> (rolls, sweep, constraints), and the current asset/editor state on this machine.
> Verdict up front: **the architecture is sound — no v2 overhaul. Fix five bugs, burn the
> graveyard, and pivot from proving the generator to building the game loop.**

---

## 1. What this project is (my understanding)

**The idea.** A persistent surface town for a dungeon crawler — not a level, a *trophy
case*. The town is assembled from flat-top hex tiles (R = 10 m); roads on tile edges are
the assembly grammar. The "Pop One" spine locks the scope to geometry: a radius-6 hex map
is 127 cells; the center 7 collapse into the Pit (the inciting wound, the dungeon mouth,
the permanent scar at (0,0)); the 120 frontier tiles each house **one soul** rescued
below. `120 + you = Pop 121 = 11²`. Aesthetic: dense turn-of-the-century London with a
quiet Colorado Front Range accent, built as *style over fidelity* — trim sheets, lighting,
modular reuse, never 120 bespoke art passes.

**The pipeline.** One Blender master (`HexBase.blend`, never saved by tooling) feeds two
tracks: *procedural* tiles baked by parametric generators (`gen_curved_tiles.py` — 14
spline-curved street configs; `gen_pit_core.py` — the 7-tile radial-star core;
`gen_buildings.py` — 12 single-story walk-in building overlays), and *bespoke* tiles
promoted into their own `.blend` (the Pit is the first). Ownership is enforced by a
git-tracked manifest; generators skip bespoke tiles. Everything exports through one
canonical glTF path to `Content/_project/HexTiles/Source/`, imports into UE under
`/Game/_project/`, and self-heals material dupes back into 4 shared masters.

**The engine.** A UE 5.7 plugin (`HexMapGenerator`): a pure, seeded growth library
(cardinal arteries off the pinned core, branching side streets, dangling-stub prune), a
tile catalog (`UHexTileSet`) covering all 14 road-edge necklaces, and the
`AHexShuffleGenerator` actor that *measures each imported mesh's true road edges from its
render vertices* and resolves rotation against geometry — making placement immune to
glTF's mirror/rotation quirks. Rolls save/load as JSON (resolved cell masks = ground
truth). Diagnostics live in editor Python: per-roll histograms, 200-roll sweeps with CSV,
and constraint-driven seed search backed by an in-engine DataTable.

**Current state.** Working end-to-end: 127 cells, 0 unresolved, buildings paired 1:1 with
road-bearing frontier cells, validated in `ShuffleLab`. The sweep proves the distribution
(Σmean = 120.0 exact, 0 unresolved over 24k cell-samples). What does *not* exist yet:
any gameplay. No tile-by-tile growth, no NPCs, no shop, no descend prompt. The plugin is
the whole codebase; the game is still a design document.

## 2. Evaluation of the idea — good, bad, ugly

### The good (genuinely strong)

- **Town-as-rescue-count is a first-class hook.** It makes meta-progression *legible* —
  the player sees the number and the skyline grow together. The on-screen label should
  eventually literally read `Pop 37 / 121`. Cheap to implement, emotionally expensive to
  the player. This is the best idea in the project and everything else correctly serves it.
- **Pop One is real scope discipline disguised as numerology.** 127/7/120/121 converts
  "how much content do we need?" from a vibe into an integer. Most solo projects die
  without that number. The asset budget analysis (~40 unique meshes) confirms it's
  achievable.
- **The 14-necklace kit is *provably complete*.** Fourteen is the full Burnside count of
  binary 6-edge necklaces — any mask the generator can emit, the kit can resolve. That's
  why 24,000 sampled cells produced zero unresolved. Most modular kits discover their
  gaps in production; yours can't have any. **Under-appreciated consequence:** this also
  makes future *player-driven incremental placement* dead-end-free — any frontier cell
  adjacent to committed edges always has a legal tile. The completeness you built to make
  the randomizer safe is exactly what makes a player-facing placement mechanic safe (§3).
- **The edge-anchor contract** (midpoint + perpendicular crossing) buying map-wide C1
  smoothness with zero runtime machinery is the correct modular design — the
  per-tile-spline pivot over the runtime spline renderer was the right rejection, made
  for the right product reason ("players get tiles that snap, not a spline toy").
- **Hexes are defensible for London, and you should own the argument:** no right angles
  means no American-grid read, and the 6-way `junction` tile is literally a **Seven
  Dials**. Lean into it — the town reads as the tangled, organically-grown London of
  Soho/Covent Garden, not Manhattan.

### The bad (design-level risks)

1. **The game-defining question is still open and everything current is downstream of the
   wrong side of it.** The randomizer rolls *whole towns*; the game places *one tile per
   rescue*. Those are different problems. "Unlock/placement: manual vs progression-driven"
   sits in Design.md's Open list like a footnote, but it decides what the generator even
   *is* (a destiny-roller, a candidate-enumerator, or a dev-only proofing tool). Every
   week of further whole-roll tuning (constraints, sweeps, bias knobs) is potentially
   polishing a dev tool, not the game. **Decide this next.** My recommendation in §3.
2. **The committed population model and the shipped metric disagree.** Design.md
   resolution #7 commits to *count, not sum* (1 tile = 1 soul), yet the code still sums
   the interim `Occupancy = (6 − roadEdges) × 2` into `EstimatedVacancies`, and
   `save_occupancy.py` exists to seed the dead metric. Saved rolls now carry
   vacancy numbers (`vac708`, `vac908`) that mean nothing under the committed design.
   Cheap fix, real conceptual debt.
3. **The buildings just shipped per-tile while CLAUDE.md's #0 next-step says per-hex
   buildings "don't fit" and footprints should be multi-hex blocks.** The repo contradicts
   itself about its own next big rock. For what it's worth I think the *shipped* answer is
   the right one (see §3.3) — but the doc conviction needs to be updated or the work
   re-justified, or future-you will re-litigate it cold.
4. **Blank volatility is a real art-direction problem, not just a stat.** σ/μ ≈ 0.43 with
   an 8%–72% open-ground swing means two players' towns (or two rolls of the same town)
   read as different *genres* — dense London vs. village green. Constraints can clamp it
   (add `blank: {max: N}` to the default DataTable), but the durable fix is the
   min-block-size rule already on the roadmap.
5. **Integration risk with the actual dungeon crawler.** Locals, shopping, descend/return
   — none of it exists here, and the dungeon game lives elsewhere. The two projects meet
   at save data, the character, and `OpenLevel`. The longer the town stays a renderer,
   the more expensive that meeting gets.

### The ugly (cosmetic but worth saying)

- **The debug skeleton is baked into the production look.** Tile-border lips are
  authored into every mesh as geometry. As a metric aid: great. But it hard-codes a
  board-game read into the art. It's on its own material (`M_TileBorder`), so the cheap
  fix is a scalar param on the master material (opacity/darkness) so the lips can fade
  for beauty shots and ship as an option, not a baked fact.
- **Hex-junction geometry will fight the Edwardian read at street level.** Every
  intersection is 60°/120°; London terraces want occasional right angles. The spline
  fanning hides much of it. Expect to need a couple of bespoke "normalizing" tiles
  (a market square, a churchyard) to break the hex rhythm where the eye lingers.

## 3. My design for the hex map

Keep ~90% of what exists. Here is what I'd commit to, in priority order:

### 3.1 Growth model: **"Destiny roll, witnessed restoration"** (with optional player agency)

Roll the full 120-cell layout **once per save** (constraint-checked), persist it via the
roll JSON you already have, then **restore one tile per rescue**:

- Restoration order = **BFS outward from the Pit along the road graph** — the town
  visibly *heals from the wound outward*, which is the story. (Ring-by-ring is the
  simpler fallback; road-graph BFS reads better because streets complete themselves.)
- Unrestored cells render as rubble/fog/footprint ghosts (one cheap overlay mesh), so the
  silhouette of the whole town is faintly present from hour one — the trophy case shows
  its empty shelves.
- **Player agency, if wanted, slots in cleanly without becoming a city-builder:** when a
  soul is rescued, highlight the 2–3 BFS-frontier candidate cells and let the player pick
  *which* lights up next, and/or which rescued soul moves into which restored building.
  Because the kit is necklace-complete, every candidate is always legal — no dead ends,
  no failure UI. Agency over *order and assignment*, not over layout. This honors
  "player-driven" without violating the scope guardrails (no combat, no sandbox).
- **Act-2 rearrange** = re-roll the *unrestored remainder* with the same constraint
  DataTable under a story justification. The engine is already runtime-callable; the
  pinned core already guarantees the Pit never moves.

This reuses every line of existing tech: `GrowRoadNetwork` (the destiny roller),
`FHexRoll` JSON (the save format *already exists*), constraints (per-save town flavor),
and the resolver (placement). The only new code is `RestoreNextTile()` — an afternoon,
not a milestone.

### 3.2 Population display: make the title the metric

Retire the occupancy sum. `PopLabel` shows **`Pop <restored+1> / 121`**. Keep `Occupancy`
in the catalog only if you want per-tile flavor ("the Hartley sisters, 2") later; it no
longer feeds the headline number. Delete `save_occupancy.py` or repurpose it.

### 3.3 Buildings: per-tile facades were right; blocks are a landmark tool

The combinatorics already voted: per-tile buildings = 12 meshes; multi-hex block
footprints = a combinatorial explosion of block shapes the road graph can carve
(unbounded authoring). Dense-London continuity is achievable *per-tile* by attacking the
visible seams instead:

- **Per-edge facade variation** via the `M_BuildingBack` seam you already designed —
  engine-side hide/replace per neighbor type. That was the right architecture; finish it.
- **Parapet/party-wall trick:** Victorian terraces read as continuous because of shared
  party walls and repeated bays, not because buildings are large. A narrow party-wall
  pilaster at each tile boundary where two building tiles abut would sell continuity for
  one extra mesh.
- **Multi-hex bespoke only for landmarks** (3–5 total): church, market hall, terminus
  hotel by the Pit. These also fix the hex-rhythm problem (§2-ugly) and give arteries
  destinations.

### 3.4 Interiors: 14 shells × dressing, one proof first

The road-config taxonomy already organizes interiors (corner shop, through-terrace,
dead-end house). Commit to: shell = the existing building mesh; dressing = a per-soul
prop set + trim-sheet materials; light = one warm interior light rig per shell. **Build
exactly one** complete soul (rescue → restore → walk in → talk → buy) before scaling
anything. That slice is the whole game in miniature and will reprice every estimate.

### 3.5 Rivers: keep deferred, but pre-commit to **layered masks**

When water comes, do *not* widen `EHexEdgeSocket` into a ternary mask — 3-state necklaces
balloon the catalog from 14 to ~130 classes. Instead give each cell **two parallel 6-bit
masks** (road, water): the growth lib runs a water pass first, the road pass treats water
edges as constraints, and only genuine bridge/bank tiles key on (road × water) pairs —
a sparse handful, not a cross-product. Your mask-rotation machinery works unchanged on
each layer independently.

### 3.6 The Pit beats more generator work

The asymmetric collapsed rim, the descent geometry, and the "Ready to descend?" →
`OpenLevel` prompt are the *game's* front door and the only level-transition in the loop.
The generator is good enough today; the Pit's art/gameplay pass is now the critical path.

## 4. Codebase evaluation

### What works (keep, and protect)

| Thing | Why it's good |
| --- | --- |
| `HexCoordinates.h` | Minimal, correct, allocation-free; exact basis-vector axial→world (no √3 accumulation); mask rotation is two shifts. |
| `HexMapGrowthLibrary` | Pure, seeded, deterministic, zero engine-world deps — exactly the shape gameplay reuse needs. The pinned-core membership model (explicit set, not radius) was the right call. |
| **Measure-and-rotate calibration** (`MeasureTileMask`) | The crown jewel. Reading `M_Road` render verts at edge midpoints + popcount sanity + authored-socket fallback turns gotchas #10/#16 (importer mirror) from a permanent landmine into a non-issue. |
| Roll save/load | Resolved-mask network as ground truth, params as provenance — the right durable format, and accidentally already the game's save format. |
| Manifest-enforced procedural/bespoke ownership | Professional-grade pipeline discipline; many studio pipelines never solve "the generator clobbered my hand work." |
| `tile_registry` / `tile_common` | Single source of tile truth pulled from the generators; ONE canonical glTF export. No second list to drift. |
| `gen_buildings.py` | Genuinely production-quality procedural mesh code: `orient(CCW)` discipline, run-based door planning (the banana lintel), weld-then-bevel, slot architecture designed for engine seams. |
| The gotchas log (29 entries) | The project's most valuable artifact after the code. Diagnostics + fixes + *why*, written for future-you. Keep feeding it. |
| `Build.ps1` | Right fix for the MSBuild `SetEnv` trap, machine-portable (derives repo from script location — notably better than the docs' hardcoded paths). |

### Bugs found (all verified by reading, ranked)

1. **`constrained_randomize` places a roll it never validated** —
   [roll_diagnostics.py:650](Content/Python/roll_diagnostics.py:650). The tool validates
   `try_seed`, then does `set_editor_property("seed", try_seed)` + `gen.randomize()` —
   but `Randomize()` **pre-increments** (`++Seed`,
   [HexShuffleGenerator.cpp:396](Plugins/HexMapGenerator/Source/HexMapGenerator/Private/HexShuffleGenerator.cpp:396)),
   so the level gets **`try_seed + 1`'s un-checked roll**. With the default constraints
   (junction ≥ 1: mean 1.65, P(0) ≈ 30%; tee_c ≥ 1: mean 0.97, P(0) ≈ 35%), the placed
   roll likely *violates* the constraints roughly half the time. Fix: add a
   `BuildFromSeed(int32)` UFUNCTION (no increment) and call it from Python; keep
   `Randomize()` as the reroll button.
2. **Both "unknown necklace name" warning paths crash** —
   [roll_diagnostics.py:446](Content/Python/roll_diagnostics.py:446) and
   [:492](Content/Python/roll_diagnostics.py:492):
   `", ".join(sorted(NECKLACE_NAMES.values()))` joins *tuples* → `TypeError`. A misspelled
   DataTable row name kills the tool instead of printing the helpful message. Fix:
   `sorted(v[0] for v in NECKLACE_NAMES.values())`.
3. **"Closest miss" reports the *latest* miss, not the closest** —
   [roll_diagnostics.py:656](Content/Python/roll_diagnostics.py:656):
   `if prev is None or abs(observed - prev[0]) > 0` overwrites on any *different* value.
   Fix: compute distance-to-satisfaction and keep the minimum.
4. **`diagnose_last_roll` ignores the property that was exposed for it** —
   [roll_diagnostics.py:199](Content/Python/roll_diagnostics.py:199) still re-grows from
   Seed with a comment claiming `LastNetwork` "can't be seen by reflection" — but the
   header *has* the `BlueprintReadOnly + AllowPrivateAccess` exposure
   ([HexShuffleGenerator.h:142](Plugins/HexMapGenerator/Source/HexMapGenerator/Public/HexShuffleGenerator.h:142));
   that exposure is the entire story of gotchas #23/#24 (it cost a full rebuild). The
   LoadRoll edge case the comment apologizes for only exists because the TODO was never
   finished. Read `last_network` directly.
5. **The retired spline-road system still auto-fires** —
   [HexShuffleGenerator.cpp:537](Plugins/HexMapGenerator/Source/HexMapGenerator/Private/HexShuffleGenerator.cpp:537):
   `if (RoadSegmentMesh) { BuildSplineRoads(); }` in `Build()`. PerTileSplineRoads.md
   explicitly lists this one-click auto-draw as RETIRED; it's inert only as long as nobody
   assigns the property. Delete the call (and ideally quarantine
   `ASplineRoad`/`bGroundOnly`/`GroundMesh`/`SplineRoadZ` behind an editor-only debug
   header or remove them).

### Smaller defects / drift

- **Comment contradicts code** in the prune pass —
  [HexMapGrowthLibrary.cpp:198](Plugins/HexMapGenerator/Source/HexMapGenerator/Private/HexMapGrowthLibrary.cpp:198)
  says "Decided against a snapshot" directly above `const TMap … Snap = Mask;` (a
  snapshot). The *code* is right.
- **Dead field:** `FHexTileDef::Weight` is never read anywhere. Delete it until variant
  pools exist (it'll return as seeded weighted selection in `FindPlacement`).
- **Placeholder-core vacancies leak:** with no `CoreLayout` assigned, placeholder core
  cells aren't in `CoreMesh`, fall through to the frontier resolver, and *do* count
  occupancy — the exclusion only works in authored-core mode. Moot once §3.2 lands.
- **`MeasureTileMask` tolerance scales with R** (`NearDist = 0.30 × CircumradiusUU` = 3 m
  at R=10) while the road cross-section is absolute (HW = 1.5 m). Works today; a future
  scale bump quietly loosens the test. Make it absolute (e.g. `HW×100 + 50`).
- **Doc drift, door rule:** Docs/Buildings.md + the function docstring say "< 3 m → 0
  doors"; the code threshold is `DOOR_W + 0.4` = **1.3 m**
  ([gen_buildings.py:229](Tools/blender/gen_buildings.py:229)).
- **Doc drift, gen_buildings module docstring:** still describes waist-height garden
  walls and a 4-slot material list (`M_GardenWall`); gardens emit zero geometry and there
  are 5 slots including `M_BuildingInterior`/`M_DoorFrame`.
- **Doc drift, CLAUDE.md:** gotcha #7 claims Source_Art/README's neighbor offsets are
  "still listed at the old R=5" — the README was already updated to R=10. The drift note
  has itself drifted. Also `sweep` provenance shows the *live* tuning (RoadBudget 174,
  BranchChance 0.21, StraightBias 0.72…) lives only on the ShuffleLab actor instance —
  worth recording the blessed tuning in a doc or making them the C++ defaults.
- **Machine portability:** every command example in CLAUDE.md/Docs hardcodes
  `C:\SCAD\_projects\…` (machine A); this machine is `C:\SCAD\Projects\…`. Build.ps1 gets
  it right by deriving paths. Docs should go repo-relative (`& .\Tools\Build.ps1` style).
- **Fresh-clone cold start:** `Content/_project/HexTiles/Source/` (the .glb staging dir)
  is gitignored and *absent* on a new machine — Tools ▸ Reimport Hex Tiles silently
  matches zero meshes until the Blender generators are re-baked. One line in the resume
  section would save the next cold start some confusion.
- **The master is polluted — and the pollution is committed** *(found live via Blender
  MCP after the initial audit pass)*: `HexBase.blend` at HEAD contains three generator
  output objects in the scene root — `T_bldg_penta`, `T_bldg_straight`, `T_bldg_tee_a`
  (mesh data names match `gen_buildings.build_walls` output exactly). Committed in
  `8e7b862` ("Buildings: ship…", 2026-06-07) — the byte-identical invariant (gotcha
  #2/#18) broke during the buildings session and went unnoticed. Functional impact is
  low (headless generators `reset()` them away; no hand edits at risk), but: the addon's
  **Build Enabled** doesn't `reset()` — building `T_bldg_penta` for preview will
  name-collide into `T_bldg_penta.001`; any future deliberate master save carries the
  stowaways forward; and the invariant is only as good as its enforcement. Fix: delete
  the three objects + orphan meshes, save the master once (deliberately), commit.
  Prevention: a pre-commit check or a `reset()`-style guard in the addon's build path
  that warns when non-CANON objects exist in the master outside `TilePreview`.

## 6. Verdict

**No v2.** The expensive, hard-to-rediscover things — the measured-rotation resolver, the
pure growth core, the anchor contract, the manifest ownership model, the necklace-complete
kit, 29 gotchas of negative knowledge — are all correct and all working. A rewrite would
spend the project's best asset to buy back problems it already solved. The genuine faults
are a half-day of diagnostics bugs, a day of graveyard hygiene, and one overdue *decision*
(the growth model) that the next phase must not start without.

The risk to manage isn't technical debt; it's **direction debt** — the generator is now
better instrumented than the game is defined. Roll destiny once, restore it one soul at a
time, and let the Pit's front door open.
